The model can say, for every system, ours or a vendor's, which people can help with it and for which task. With that in the model, it answers:

- whom to contact about an incident, an API or contract change, an upgrade, or a change to review;
- who has the vendor account, the keys and the billing, or grants access to our own system;
- who built the integration, and so can say why it works the way it does;
- who has worked on a system, to review a change or join the work on it;
- which systems and containers use a system, and would be affected by a change to it;
- how long ago someone checked the people for a system;
- [which systems have lost most of the people who know them](../recipes/people-who-left/).

## What is modeled

Each system has four people fields and a date, as metadata:

| Field | Who | Ask them when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `integratedBy` | integrated it originally | you need to know why it works the way it does |
| `experts` | know best how it works and how its integration is changed | there is an incident, an API or contract change, an upgrade, or a change to review |
| `contributors` | have worked on it | you need reviewers or extra hands, or the experts are away |
| `administrators` | manage it: for a vendor, the account, keys, billing and support; for our own, its accounts and access | credentials need rotating, someone needs access, or a support ticket or plan change is due |
| `peopleConfirmed` | | the date someone last checked the four fields |

The people come from one list, `model/people.json`, as a handle and a name. Someone who has left stays in the list, marked with the date they left. Which systems and containers use a system isn't a field: it comes from the model's relationships.

## Why not one owner

Documentation goes out of date, and the names of people go out of date first: teams reorganize, people change roles and leave. A field like "Owner: Payments team" stays correct for longer only because it says so little. When a payment provider's webhooks fail at night, it doesn't say whom to call.

The person who manages a vendor account is often not the one who knows how to change the integration, and neither of them may have built it. A question sent to "the owner" usually gets passed on until it reaches one of these people; the fields name them directly. The `owner` field stays, for the team that is accountable for the system.

External systems have the same fields as our own, because what we know about them is known only by our own people: who connected it, who can debug it, who has the login to the vendor's account. The vendor's documentation doesn't contain any of that.

## How the fields stay accurate

- **Contributors are a longer list than experts.** When the experts have moved on, someone among the contributors can usually say whom to ask next.
- **The date shows how old the fields are.** When `peopleConfirmed` is more than about six months old, the coding agent asks you to check them the next time you work on the system with it.
- **Each person is listed once.** Renaming someone is one edit to `model/people.json`, and marking someone as having left doesn't touch the systems they worked on.
- **Git proposes contributors, and you confirm them.** The authors of the code that integrates a system are a good first guess, but having changed a file doesn't mean someone understands the system, so the agent adds only the people you confirm.

To model them, say "Help me document Payments" to a coding agent with the [plugin](../modeling/with-an-agent/) installed: its `document-a-system` skill asks for them after the system's users and the systems it talks to. To show them on a page, use the [editable-metadata](../addons/editable-metadata/#people) addon.
